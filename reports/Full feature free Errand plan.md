# Rebuild nearly all of rtrvr for $0

Errand can cover every remaining rtrvr feature at $0 except two. The first is **a WhatsApp channel that is guaranteed free**: Meta began charging for most replies on October 1, 2026, so Errand uses a free Telegram bot instead. The second is **browsers that keep working while your laptop is switched off**: no card-free, always-on server exists in 2026, so your laptop has to be the "cloud". Everything else fits on three free pieces. **Your laptop** runs the browsers, the 53 local Qwen3.5-2B brains, the scheduler, the API, the MCP server, the CLI and the recordings. **A free Cloudflare Worker** is the only part that never sleeps. It holds incoming triggers until the laptop collects them, and it hides your Gemini key for the helper on your website. **Tailscale's free personal plan** lets your phone watch and take over a running browser. On the brain side, Gemini 3.8 Flash does the planning and the hard steps on public pages, Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite is the backup, and the 2B does the bulk reading, every private page, and checks the others' work. The biggest limit is not a missing feature. It is one shared Gemini allowance per Google project, and Google shows its size only inside your AI Studio account. So Errand has to budget that allowance, fall back to the local model when it runs out, and never get around it with extra keys. The helper on your website comes with one more limit: on the free tier, it may not be offered to visitors in the EU (and the rest of the EEA), the UK or Switzerland.

All facts are as of October 19, 2026. "Measured" means a test on your laptop (RTX 3060 6 GB, 16 GB RAM). "Estimate" or "design" means it was worked out and not tested.

## The laptop becomes the cloud browser, watched from your phone over Tailscale

rtrvr runs its browsers on Google Cloud Run, with Gemini Flash doing the thinking ([rtrvr Google Cloud page](https://rtrvr.ai/google-cloud-partner)). Its "53 agents" demo ran 53 cloud browsers over 105 Product Hunt pages in 184 seconds. The demo showed a live grid that a human could take over, and it also advertised built-in CAPTCHA solving and scaling to 150+ browsers ([rtrvr video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ggLDvZKuBlU)). Errand copies the useful parts on your own machine. Chrome has a built-in feature called the DevTools "screencast" (`Page.startScreencast`). It sends a stream of small JPEG pictures of the page and lets you set the quality, the size and how many frames to send ([Chrome DevTools Protocol](https://chromedevtools.github.io/devtools-protocol/tot/Page/)). Errand passes those pictures to a web page on your phone. When you tap or type on the phone, Errand sends those actions back into the browser. That is the takeover. The screencast also works on hidden ("headless") browsers, which matters when jobs run while you are logged off. Chrome marks the feature as experimental, so keep Chrome's version fixed and fall back to one screenshot a second if an update breaks it.

**Tailscale is the safe way to reach it from your phone.** Its Personal plan is "$0 Free forever", with unlimited devices and up to 6 users. It is meant for personal, non-commercial use, such as a homelab ([Tailscale pricing](https://tailscale.com/pricing)). It creates a private network between your phone and laptop, so nothing is open to the public internet. The other options fall short. Cloudflare Quick Tunnels do not support one common type of live update (Server-Sent Events) and allow at most 200 requests at a time ([Cloudflare Quick Tunnels](https://developers.cloudflare.com/tunnel/get-started/quick-tunnels/)). Cloudflare's terms also require paid services "to serve video and other large files" through its network ([Cloudflare terms](https://www.cloudflare.com/service-specific-terms-application-services/)), and a live screen stream is close to video. ngrok's free plan allows only 1 GB a month of outgoing data ([ngrok limits](https://ngrok.com/docs/pricing-limits/free-plan-limits)). A live view at about 50 KB a frame and 5 frames a second uses roughly 0.9 GB an hour (estimate), so ngrok's month would be gone in about an hour.

| | Browser runs with live view and takeover |
|---|---|
| **$0 route** | Playwright Chromium (plus Errand's Scrapling fetch ladder) on the laptop. The screencast goes over a WebSocket to a phone page on Tailscale. Taps and keys go back as browser input. Errand's page listens only on the Tailscale network and has its own login as a second lock. |
| **Limits** | About 12–20 tabs loading at once on 6 CPU cores (estimate). The 53 brains are measured, but 53 browsers are not. Each live view uses about 0.9 GB an hour (estimate). The screencast is experimental. The laptop must be on, plugged in and online. |
| **Different from rtrvr** | Your home internet connection and your laptop instead of Google Cloud. A grid of the 12–20 active tabs, not 53+ separate cloud browsers. Errand stops at bot challenges instead of solving them. |
| **Not possible at $0** | Browsers that run while the laptop is off. Scaling to 150+ browsers. CAPTCHA solving. In your own test, the stealth browser failed Cloudflare's Turnstile challenge 10 times out of 10, and Errand's spec turns solving off ([bench results](../bench/results.md)). Use takeover for logins, two-step codes and decisions. Do not use it to beat a site's bot check so the bot can carry on. |

## Task Scheduler and a Cloudflare inbox replace paid schedules and triggers

On rtrvr, "schedules, triggers, cookie sync, and running rtrvr's agent in your Chrome through API or MCP require a paid plan", starting at $9.99 a month ([rtrvr pricing](https://rtrvr.ai/pricing)). Errand does the same jobs with Windows' own tools. Task Scheduler starts the Errand engine when you log on. Its "wake the computer to run this task" setting (`WakeToRun`) can wake a sleeping PC, even if the screen stays off ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/taskschd/tasksettings-waketorun)). Inside the engine, a Python scheduler that saves jobs in a small database (for example APScheduler with SQLite) runs the hourly and daily jobs. It also catches up on any runs it missed when the laptop starts up.

A trigger is an outside event that starts a job, such as a form being sent or a service sending a "webhook" (an automatic web message). It needs something that is always online. **The free Cloudflare Worker is that piece.** Workers Free gives "100,000 per day" requests and 10 ms of CPU time per request. Its D1 database allows 100,000 rows written a day and 5 GB of storage ([Cloudflare Workers pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/pricing/)). The Worker checks a secret and saves each event as a row. The laptop asks for new events about once a minute, which uses about 1,440 requests a day, then deletes the ones it has handled. Events wait safely over an offline weekend. Do not use Cloudflare Queues as the store, because free messages are deleted after 24 hours ([Cloudflare Queues pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/queues/platform/pricing/)). Do not use KV either, because it allows only 1,000 writes a day ([Cloudflare KV pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/kv/platform/pricing/)). Monitoring, meaning "tell me when this page changes", is just a scheduled replay (see the recordings section). Errand compares the new result with the last one and sends you a Telegram or Slack message when something changes. If the saved recipe still works, this uses no AI at all.

| | Schedules, triggers and monitoring |
|---|---|
| **$0 route** | Task Scheduler with `WakeToRun` starts the engine. APScheduler with SQLite times the jobs. A Cloudflare Worker with D1 collects webhooks. The laptop collects them about once a minute. Monitoring is a scheduled replay plus a "what changed" check. |
| **Limits** | The laptop must be plugged in, set to "never sleep when plugged in", and not shut down. Wake timers are less reliable on "Modern Standby" laptops. Windows Update restarts can kill long runs. The Worker can only receive and store events, because 10 ms of CPU is far too little to run a browser or an AI. |
| **Different from rtrvr** | rtrvr's schedules run in its cloud whether or not your computer is on, and they cost $9.99 a month or more. Errand's are free but tied to your laptop. Triggers that arrive while the laptop is off are kept and run later. They do not run straight away. |
| **Not possible at $0** | Runs that happen on time while the laptop is shut down. No card-free, always-on server is left: Render's free services "sleep after 15 idle minutes", Railway gives $1 of credit a month, and Fly.io requires a card ([flaviocopes free-tier survey](https://flaviocopes.com/hosting-free-tiers/)). Keeping a sleeping free service awake with "pinger" tricks goes against what those providers intend. |

## Sheets, Drive, Slack and Notion stay free under per-minute limits

All four connections are free if you create your own "app" with each service once. Google Sheets allows 60 reads and 60 writes a minute per user. It has "no limit to the number of requests" per day, and normal use is free. Google does say that going over the quota "is planned to incur charges... later in 2026" ([Sheets API limits](https://developers.google.com/workspace/sheets/api/limits)). Drive now counts usage in "quota units". Listing files costs 100 units, against an allowance of 325,000 units a minute per user ([Drive API limits](https://developers.google.com/workspace/drive/api/guides/limits)). To connect your account, use a "Desktop app" sign-in client, which Google designs for apps installed on your own computer. One catch: while your Google project is left in "Testing" mode, Google issues "a refresh token expiring in 7 days" ([Google OAuth 2.0](https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2)). In plain terms, you have to sign in again once a week. Testing mode allows up to 100 test users ([Google Cloud Help](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/15549945)). The alternative is to publish the app without Google's review and click past the "unverified app" warning yourself. That probably avoids the weekly sign-in, but nobody has tested it yet.

Slack is the easiest. Create an app for your own workspace, turn on "Incoming Webhooks" and pick a channel. Each webhook posts to one fixed channel and cannot delete messages ([Slack webhooks](https://docs.slack.dev/messaging/sending-messages-using-incoming-webhooks)). Both webhooks and bot messages allow about one message a second ([Slack rate limits](https://docs.slack.dev/apis/web-api/rate-limits)). Notion allows 180 requests a minute (about 3 a second) on plans below Business. Each text item can be at most 2,000 characters, and each request at most 1,000 blocks or 500 KB. A new "Free workspace block limit" started on September 8, 2026, but the page does not give its size ([Notion request limits](https://developers.notion.com/reference/request-limits)).

| | Google Sheets and Drive | Slack | Notion |
|---|---|---|---|
| **$0 route** | Your own Google Cloud project with a Desktop sign-in client. Ask only for access to files Errand creates (`drive.file`) to keep the warnings down. Add many rows in one request. | An internal app in your own workspace: a webhook for alerts, or a bot token when you need to choose channels or use threads | An internal integration token, with your results pages shared to it |
| **Limits** | 60 writes a minute per user; weekly sign-in in Testing mode | About 1 message a second; one channel per webhook | About 3 requests a second; split long text into 2,000-character pieces; write one page per run, not one block per row |
| **Different from rtrvr** | You do the one-time setup yourself. Your data goes straight from your laptop to your own accounts, with no middleman. | Same | Same |
| **Not possible at $0** | Going past the quota once Google starts charging for that, because Errand should never be linked to a billing account. Throttle well below the limit instead. | Unknown: whether Slack's free plan still caps how many apps a workspace can have (it used to be 10) | Unlimited logging, if the new Free block limit turns out to be small. The page also does not confirm that the Free plan includes API access. |

## One Python install gives the API, the MCP server and the CLI

These three are different doors into the same engine. An **API** lets other programs send Errand jobs. An **MCP server** lets AI assistants such as Claude use Errand as a tool. A **CLI** lets you type commands like `errand run "find cheap flights"` in a terminal. The official MCP Python SDK installs with `pip install "mcp[cli]"` and needs Python 3.11 or newer ([MCP Python SDK](https://github.com/modelcontextprotocol/python-sdk)). The simplest setup is "stdio", where the assistant starts Errand itself as a background program, with no ports and no passwords. In Claude Code, one command adds it (`claude mcp add --scope user errand -- python -m errand.mcp_server`). The older "SSE" connection type is deprecated ([Claude Code MCP docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/mcp)). On Windows, Claude Desktop reads its list of servers from `%APPDATA%\Claude\claude_desktop_config.json`, and you must fully quit and restart Desktop after editing it ([Connect local MCP servers](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/docs/develop/connect-local-servers)). If several programs need to share one running Errand, the SDK can serve over the web at `http://localhost:8000/mcp`. One rule for stdio: Errand must never print logs to normal output, because that output is reserved for the MCP messages. Send logs to the error stream instead. The CLI needs no extra library. Python's built-in `argparse` handles subcommands ([Python argparse](https://docs.python.org/3/library/argparse.html)), and `errand mcp` can start the MCP server, so there is only one thing to install.

| | API, MCP server and CLI |
|---|---|
| **$0 route** | A local web API on the laptop. The official MCP SDK as a stdio server for Claude Code and Claude Desktop, with a web option on localhost. An `argparse` CLI installed as the `errand` command. |
| **Limits** | Reachable from your phone only through Tailscale. Outside services cannot call it directly; they post to the Worker inbox, and the laptop picks the job up later. |
| **Different from rtrvr** | On rtrvr, controlling its agent through the API or MCP is paid. Only its browser tools, which let your own AI drive Chrome, are free ([rtrvr pricing](https://rtrvr.ai/pricing)). On Errand, all of it is free. |
| **Not possible at $0** | A public API that other people can use and that is always up. The Worker can accept jobs at any hour, but it cannot run them. |

## Recordings replay 105 pages in 0.42 seconds with no AI

rtrvr's agent is driven by AI throughout. Its documentation describes a planner directing "20+ specialized sub-agents" ([rtrvr docs](https://www.rtrvr.ai/docs/web-agent)). Errand's "record once" feature does the opposite: AI is used once, and the replays use none. Playwright's recorder already shows the right way to save steps. It notes which element you clicked by its role, its visible text or its test label, rather than by its fragile position on the page. It also keeps refining each locator until it matches exactly one element ([Playwright codegen](https://playwright.dev/docs/codegen)). Errand saves each step as a short record: what you meant, which page, what you did, several ways to find the element, and a check that the step worked. A replay tries each way of finding the element in turn, with no AI. Only when they all fail does it ask the local 2B, "which of these elements matches what the user meant?". If the 2B can't tell, it asks Gemini. The answer that works is saved back into the recording. Stagehand ([Stagehand caching](https://www.mintlify.com/browserbase/stagehand/concepts/caching)) and Qodex ([Qodex replay cache](https://qodex.ai/docs/ui-testing-replay-cache)) use the same "fix one step, then save it" pattern. Your own test shows both sides of this. **A recipe written by one AI call replayed across all 105 pages in 0.42 seconds with zero AI calls.** The fields it got right, it got right on every page, but it invented two selectors that were wrong on every page ([bench results](../bench/results.md)). That is why every replay needs a check and a fixer step.

To record while you are logged in, Playwright can save your session (cookies and site storage) to a file such as `auth.json`. That file is sensitive and must never be shared or uploaded. Chrome 136 and newer block automation of your normal profile, so record in a separate Errand profile instead ([Playwright codegen](https://playwright.dev/docs/codegen)).

| | Record once and replay |
|---|---|
| **$0 route** | Record steps with several ways to find each element. Replay with no AI. For a broken step, ask the 2B first, then Gemini, and save the fix. Private recordings are fixed by the 2B only. |
| **Limits** | A recording reflects one session, and Playwright's docs say to review it. Site redesigns break steps. There is no independent data on how often automatic fixing works, and the browser-use team calls its own version "very early" ([workflow-use](https://github.com/cdxiaodong/workflow-use)). |
| **Different from rtrvr** | A healthy replay costs no AI allowance and takes seconds, instead of re-running an AI agent each time. |
| **Not possible at $0** | Replaying through bot challenges. Guaranteed self-repair after a big site redesign: record again instead. |

## Telegram stands in for WhatsApp; the feed and skills stay local

WhatsApp is the one channel that is no longer reliably free. Meta charges per delivered message. Replies inside the 24-hour window after you message the business became charged on October 1, 2026, after the first 1,000 a month per number. Meta also tells businesses to make sure they have a payment method on file, and its wording about what happens without one contradicts itself ([WhatsApp pricing](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing)). Meta's free test number can message only up to 5 verified numbers and is meant for development ([WhatsApp Cloud API Get Started](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/cloud-api/get-started/)). Unofficial tools that drive your personal WhatsApp, such as whatsapp-web.js or Baileys, go against WhatsApp's terms on automated access and can get your account banned ([WhatsApp Terms](https://www.whatsapp.com/legal/terms-of-service)), so Errand leaves them out. **A Telegram bot is the free replacement.** You create it with @BotFather. The laptop asks Telegram for new messages, so no public web address is needed. Telegram asks bots to send no more than about 1 message a second to any one chat ([Telegram Bots FAQ](https://core.telegram.org/bots/faq)), which is plenty for one person.

The "For You" feed and the skills library need no cloud at all. A **skill** is a recording with blanks to fill in (for example `{search_term}`), plus a plain-English description that the planner uses if the replay breaks. So the skills library is just a folder of recordings. The **For You** feed suggests things to run, using your recent recordings, run history and schedules. The local 2B ranks the ideas, and at most one Gemini call a day turns them into nicer wording. Keep your browsing history out of that call, because free-tier Gemini prompts can be read by human reviewers ([Gemini API terms](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms)).

| | WhatsApp-style channel | "For You" feed | Skills library |
|---|---|---|---|
| **$0 route** | Telegram bot. WhatsApp only as an optional add-on, on the test number, at your own risk. | Built from local run history and ranked by the 2B; at most 1 Gemini call a day | Recordings with blanks, saved as local files |
| **Limits** | About 1 message a second per chat; Telegram does not publish exact limits | Only as good as your own history | Skills break when sites change, like recordings |
| **Different from rtrvr** | You chat with Errand in Telegram, not WhatsApp | Suggestions come from your own data, which never leaves the laptop | Personal library; share a skill by sending its file |
| **Not possible at $0** | A WhatsApp channel that is guaranteed free and allowed by its terms over the long run | Suggestions based on what other users do | A shared online skills store, which would need a server |

## The website helper runs on Cloudflare and Gemini only, outside Europe

A helper like rtrvr's Rover on your own website has to answer visitors when your laptop is off, so it cannot use the 2B live. The $0 setup is a small script on your site that talks to a Cloudflare Worker. The Worker holds your Gemini key as a hidden "secret" and passes questions to Gemini. Workers Free allows 100,000 requests a day, 10 ms of CPU per request and 128 MB of memory ([Workers limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/limits/)). Waiting for Gemini to answer counts as waiting, not as CPU work, so a light proxy should stay under the 10 ms CPU limit (estimate; confirm it with Cloudflare's live logs). Hiding the key matters, because Google says credentials must be kept confidential, and "may not be embedded in open source projects" ([Google APIs Terms](https://developers.google.com/terms)). The 2B still helps, offline. It reads your site's pages ahead of time and saves the facts as a small file that the Worker includes with each question.

Google's terms allow you to offer an app like this to users in supported regions, with four conditions ([Gemini API terms](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms)). **In the EU (and the rest of the EEA), the UK and Switzerland, "you may use only Paid Services"**, so hide the helper from visitors there. Cloudflare reports each visitor's country, but this was not re-checked. The helper must not be aimed at under-18s. Your site needs a privacy policy ([Google APIs Terms](https://developers.google.com/terms)). And visitors must be told that their chats go to Google and may be read by reviewers. The helper also uses the same Gemini allowance as Errand, because limits are counted "per project, not per API key" ([Gemini rate limits](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/rate-limits)). So the Worker must protect it. Accept requests only from your own domain, cap requests per visitor, limit question length, and keep a fixed set of instructions that visitors cannot change.

| | Helper on your own website |
|---|---|
| **$0 route** | Script on your site → Cloudflare Worker (key kept as a secret) → Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite. Answers draw on site facts the 2B prepared ahead of time. |
| **Limits** | 100,000 Worker requests a day. Its Gemini use comes out of Errand's allowance, so give it a hard daily cap. Visitors must not type personal data. |
| **Different from rtrvr** | It answers from your site's own content, with no live laptop brain behind it. You run it and pay nothing. |
| **Not possible at $0** | Serving visitors in the EU, UK or Switzerland. Live answers from the local 2B, which would mean opening your laptop to the internet. High traffic beyond Gemini's free allowance. |

## Gemini plans, the 2B does the bulk, and code checks both

The quality boost comes from giving each brain the job it is good at. On the free tier, **Gemini 3.8 Flash** is the best planner. It is stable, reads up to 1,048,576 tokens, and supports tool calls, fixed-format answers and a preview of computer use ([Gemini 3.8 Flash](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/models/gemini-3.8-flash)). Google recommends "3.5 Flash-Lite or 3.8 Flash" for new projects ([Gemini models](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/models)). Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview and the new Gemini 4 Argon are not free ([Gemini pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)). Gemini also offers an OpenAI-style endpoint (still marked beta), so one piece of code can talk to both Gemini and your local llama-server by switching the address ([Gemini OpenAI compatibility](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/openai)). The local **Qwen3.5-2B** proved itself on bulk form-filling. At 53 slots it read 2.66 pages a second, with 92.6% of fields correct (not counting a star rating that never appears as text) and no broken answers. Its real mistakes were rare but real: twice it returned a sentence from a book's description as the title ([bench results](../bench/results.md)). It is weak at long, multi-step tasks, scoring 48.8 on the TAU2 multi-step test in its makers' own report ([Qwen3.5-2B card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-2B)). Published hybrid designs share one pattern: the local model goes first, a check decides, and only the failures go up to the cloud ([Strathweb](https://strathweb.com/2025/12/slm-default-llm-fallback-pattern-with-agent-framework-and-azure-ai-foundry)).

| Job | Who does it | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Turn your request into a plan | Gemini 3.8 Flash | Strongest free planner |
| Choose the next click on multi-step or unfamiliar public sites | Gemini 3.8 Flash | The 2B is weak at multi-step tasks |
| Anything logged in, personal, banking or email | **Qwen3.5-2B only** | Free-tier Gemini data can be reviewed by people and used for training ([Gemini API terms](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms)) |
| Bulk reading (one page, one small form) | 2B, 53 slots for batches, about 35 when you are waiting | Measured 2.66 pages a second at 53 slots. About 35 slots should give about 96% of that speed with about 13-second answers (calculated). |
| Check answers | 2B plus plain code | Answers must be valid, required fields filled, and two slots must agree |
| Fix a failed page | 2B retry, then Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite | Send only the rows that failed |
| Write recipes, skills and recording fixes | Gemini 3.8 Flash for public sites, the 2B for private ones | One call per site layout, then free replays |
| "For You" wording and the website helper | Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite | Keeps them away from the planner's per-model limit |

When Gemini's allowance runs out, it answers with error 429, and the daily count resets at midnight Pacific time ([Gemini rate limits](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/rate-limits)). Errand then steps down a ladder. **First, 3.8 Flash work moves to 3.5 Flash-Lite**, which has its own per-model limit. **Next, if that is used up too, the 2B takes over in "simple mode"**: single-page tasks only, with a warning to you. Multi-step tasks wait in a queue until the reset. Errand never switches to a second key or project. How big the allowance is remains the biggest unknown. Google shows free limits only inside your AI Studio account. Recent outside reports range from about 20 to about 1,500 requests a day per Flash model ([klymentiev](https://klymentiev.com/blog/gemini-api-free-tier); [aifreeapi](https://www.aifreeapi.com/en/posts/google-gemini-api-free-tier)), and users reported limits that would not reset in mid-October ([Google AI forum](https://discuss.ai.google.dev/tag/rate-limits/415)). So Errand should count its own Gemini calls and split the day's allowance up front. The split below is a suggested starting point.

| Gemini use | Model | Suggested share of the daily allowance |
|---|---|---|
| Planning and hard steps | 3.8 Flash | Most of 3.8 Flash's allowance |
| Fixing failed rows and broken recording steps | 3.5 Flash-Lite | About half of Flash-Lite's allowance |
| Website helper | 3.5 Flash-Lite | A hard daily cap, about a third of Flash-Lite's allowance |
| "For You" | 3.5 Flash-Lite | 1 call a day |
| Reserve | Either | Keep about 10% unused for emergencies |

## Limitations

These notes leave several gaps. The Gemini model names (3.8, 3.6 and 3.5 generation) and the free model list were read through a summarizing fetch tool, so confirm them in AI Studio before writing code. Nobody knows the exact free daily allowance until you look at your own AI Studio page. It is also unconfirmed whether 3.8 Flash's computer-use preview is free, and the 3.5 Flash-Lite model page was not read. None of these has been tested on your laptop yet: the screencast live view, takeover, self-fixing replays, a real 53-tile grid, or JavaScript-heavy pages. Your speed and accuracy numbers come from small, static practice pages of about 630 tokens. Several provider details are still open. Nobody checked whether Notion's Free plan includes API access or how big its new block limit is. Whether WhatsApp needs a card for its first 1,000 free replies is unclear. So are Slack's free-plan app cap, Cloudflare's free rate-limiting and country checks, Worker scheduled-task allowances, and Tailscale's bandwidth limits. The idea that publishing the Google app without review avoids the weekly sign-in comes from reading the documentation, not from a test. Last, the notes do not describe how rtrvr's own Rover, WhatsApp, "For You" and skills features work. So the "different from rtrvr" rows for those compare Errand with the features as you described them, not with rtrvr's documentation.

## Conclusion

Every feature on the list turns out to be cheap to build. The scarce things are two you cannot add with code: hours when the laptop is switched on, and one Google project's daily Gemini allowance. That shapes the design. Anything that must work at all hours has to be small enough to run in 10 ms on a free Worker. In practice that means only the trigger inbox and the website helper's proxy. Anything that spends Gemini calls has to have a budget. And anything that repeats should become a recording, because a recording that works costs nothing to run. Getting a "53 cloud browser" feel from one laptop is less about matching rtrvr's scale than about making sure Gemini is called only for the few steps that need it.

The privacy rule changes what "better brain quality" means. Gemini can make Errand smarter only on public pages. Signed-in and personal work, which is the most valuable thing a personal agent does, stays with the 2B, whose real weakness is multi-step work. The most useful next step is not adding more brains. It is to measure how often the 2B plus a Gemini fixer gets real multi-step tasks right on public sites. Then turn the tasks that pass into recordings, so that the private versions can replay them with no AI at all.
